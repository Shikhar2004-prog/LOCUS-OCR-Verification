The problem

An OCR engine always returns text, including when it is wrong. Tesseract read the price 6.40 as 640 and reported 0.92 confidence. Passed on to accounting, that becomes a wrong payment.

How LOCUS decides, for one word

  1. Counterfactual string graph. Around the claimed string, list what it could have been confused with: look-alike characters (0/O, 1/l, 5/S), case flips, deletions and insertions, and the readings of two frozen recognisers (TrOCR-base and PARSeq).
  2. Render-normalised evidence. For each candidate v: ρ(v) = score(v | real image) − score(v | clean rendering of v). Near 0 means the image looks as much like v as a clean print of v does. Very negative means it does not.
  3. GNN with a local-optimality readout. A graph network asks whether the claim is the best explanation among its neighbours, P_opt. Pretrained without labels.
  4. Few-shot head. 25 labelled words per class adapt it to a new document type and give P(correct).

Where it helps

Invoice and receipt capture, insurance and bank forms, healthcare and government intake, archive digitisation: anywhere a team must choose between checking every word and trusting the engine.

Benchmark results from the paper (Macro-F1 / AUROC, k = 25 labels per class)

Best value per column in green. LOCUS has the best averages. It does not win everywhere: PARSeq re-reading ties on FUNSD and is better on IIIT5K, and TrOCR re-reading has higher F1 on SROIE. The best re-reader changes from dataset to dataset; LOCUS stays near the top on all five.

Honest limitations

  • On these synthetic demo pages, flagging every word where PARSeq disagrees catches about as many errors as LOCUS (see the comparison on the first tab).
  • Substituting PARSeq's reading repairs more errors than LOCUS's repair proposal on four of five benchmarks.
  • Speed: about 18 words/s on one RTX A6000 (PARSeq alone: 372 words/s). Fine for back-office batches, not for real-time use.
  • Latin script and English benchmarks only. Words that Tesseract merges into one box are flagged but cannot be repaired word by word.

\section{Proposed Method: LOCUS}
\label{sec:method}
\textbf{Problem statement.} An OCR engine $\mathcal{E}$ maps a word (or text-line) image $x$ to a claim
$h=\mathcal{E}(x)$. The engine is a black box: we observe only $h$. With $t$ the true transcription, verification
predicts $y=\mathbf{1}[h=t]$ (\textsc{Correct} vs.\ \textsc{Incorrect}) and, optionally, proposes a repair $\hat t$.
We study the label-efficient regime with only $k\in\{5,10,25,50,100\}$ labelled words per class plus an unlabelled pool
of images with engine outputs, and also report full supervision. Figure~\ref{fig:arch} gives an overview on a real
test word and Algorithm~\ref{alg:locus} summarises inference.

\subsection{Counterfactual string graphs}
\label{sec:graph}
For each claim $h$ we build a graph $G(h)=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})$ whose nodes are strings: the claim and up to
$M=28$ counterfactual neighbours generated without any ground truth, in this priority order: (i)~the greedy readings
$g_1,g_2$ of two frozen verifier recognisers $V_1,V_2$; (ii)~\emph{reading-aligned edits}, the single substitutions,
insertions and deletions that move $h$ one step towards $g_1$ or $g_2$ under a Levenshtein alignment; (iii)~\emph{confusion
edits}: substitutions within visually confusable groups (e.g.\ \{\texttt{O,0,o,Q,D}\}, \{\texttt{I,l,1,|}\},
\{\texttt{5,S}\}), case flips and single deletions. Every neighbour is linked to the claim by an edge typed by its edit
operation (seven types); \emph{sibling} edges link neighbours that edit the same position (competing explanations of
one glyph) and \emph{path} edges link each reading to the single edits leading to it, giving a one-hot edge type
$e_{uv}\in\{0,1\}^{11}$. OCR errors are near misses, so the truth lies inside $G(h)$ for most real errors
(Table~\ref{tab:truth_in_graph}), which makes repair possible.

\subsection{Render-normalised evidence}
\label{sec:evidence}
Let $s_V(v\mid x)=\sum_i\log p_V(v_i\mid v_{<i},x)$ be the teacher-forced log-likelihood of string $v$ under a frozen
autoregressive recogniser $V$. It conflates visual fit with the prior that $V$'s decoder has learned over strings: a
correct but rare string (a serial number, a surname, lower-case text under a recogniser trained on upper-case
receipts) scores low. With $R(v)$ a clean rendering of $v$ in a standard font at the height of $x$, we define
\begin{equation}
\rho_V(v)\;=\;s_V(v\mid x)\;-\;s_V\big(v\mid R(v)\big).
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Under the factorisation $\log p_V(v\mid x)\approx\log p_{\mathrm{vis}}(x\mid v)+\log\pi_V(v)-\log Z(x)$ the string
prior $\pi_V(v)$ appears in both terms and cancels; $\rho_V(v)\approx0$ means ``$x$ is as convincing a picture of $v$
as a perfect rendering'', while strongly negative values flag visual disagreement (Figure~\ref{fig:render}). We also
record the token-level minima of the log-likelihood and of the ratio (an error is often a single character) and the
cosine distance between $V$'s decoder states on $x$ and on $R(v)$. Every quantity is computed with two recognisers of
different design: $V_1$=TrOCR-base~\cite{li2023trocr} (ViT encoder, BPE decoder; the printed, handwritten or
scene-text checkpoint matching the domain) and $V_2$=PARSeq~\cite{bautista2022parseq} (character-level permuted
autoregressive decoder). Neither is fine-tuned. Each node thus carries $2\times11$ evidence features, seven
claim-relative differences $\phi(v)-\phi(h)$, its edit type and its relative edit position.

\subsection{The LOCUS network}
\label{sec:model}
Node features are standardised with statistics of the \emph{unlabelled} pool and embedded linearly. Three
relation-aware GATv2 layers~\cite{brody2022gatv2} with edge features and residual LayerNorm update the node states:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
h_u^{(\ell+1)}&=\mathrm{LN}\Big(h_u^{(\ell)}+\sigma\Big(\textstyle\sum_{v\in\mathcal{N}(u)}\alpha_{uv}^{(\ell)}W^{(\ell)}h_v^{(\ell)}\Big)\Big),\\
\alpha_{uv}^{(\ell)}&=\mathrm{softmax}_v\Big(a^\top\mathrm{LReLU}\big(W[h_u\|h_v\|e_{uv}]\big)\Big).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Local-optimality readout.} A plausibility score $q_v=w_q^\top\mathrm{MLP}(h_v)$ defines
$P_{\mathrm{opt}}(v\mid G)=\exp q_v/\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}}\exp q_u$, the probability that $v$ is the best explanation of
$x$ within its neighbourhood. The verdict combines the claim's state, an attention pool and a max pool over its rivals,
and the log-probability that the claim itself is the local optimum,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{logit}\,P(y=1\mid x,h)=\mathrm{MLP}\big([h_h\,\|\,\textstyle\sum_v a_v h_v\,\|\,\max_v h_v]\big)+\beta\log P_{\mathrm{opt}}(h\mid G),
\end{equation}
and the same distribution gives the repair $\hat t=\arg\max_{v\neq h}P_{\mathrm{opt}}(v\mid G)$. Complementing the
string-level graph, \emph{character evidence} summarises per-position counterfactual margins of the claim (drop in
$s_{V_1}$ under confusable replacement, deletion or case flip, token entropy and token-level ratio), pooled by
mean/min/max.

\subsection{Label-free pretraining and few-shot adaptation}
\label{sec:pretrain}
On the unlabelled training images we keep those on which two OCR engines agree (a high-precision proxy for
correctness) and pair each with (i) the agreed string as a pseudo-correct claim and (ii) an OCR-style corruption of it
(confusable substitution, case flip, deletion or insertion) as a pseudo-incorrect claim whose repair is the original.
\ours{} is trained on these graphs with
$\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{BCE}\big(P(y=1\mid x,h),\tilde y\big)-\lambda\log P_{\mathrm{opt}}(\tilde t\mid G)$ ($\lambda=0.5$),
where $\tilde y$ is the pseudo-label and $\tilde t$ the node holding the pseudo-truth; no human label is used. Given
$k$ labelled words per class we (a) fine-tune the pretrained network for 300 steps (AdamW, lr $3\cdot10^{-4}$) and
(b) fit an $\ell_2$-regularised logistic regression on the frozen embedding $[z\,\|\,\log P_{\mathrm{opt}}\,\|\,\mathrm{logit}]$
and the character evidence, with regularisation chosen by cross-validation \emph{on the support set only}; the verdict
averages (a) and (b). No validation labels are used in the few-shot regime.

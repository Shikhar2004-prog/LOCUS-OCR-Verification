\subsection{Statistical significance at the document level}
Words cropped from one page share scanner, font and annotator, so a word-level bootstrap overstates confidence. For
FUNSD and CORD we recovered the source document of every crop (by replaying the seeded crop selection, verified against
the released manifest with zero mismatches) and resample whole \emph{documents} (50 test forms, 100 test receipts).
IIIT5K has one word per image, so word-level resampling is exact; the public SROIE and IAM releases carry no document
identifiers, so their intervals are word-level and may be optimistic (Table~\ref{tab:significance}). \SIGSENTENCE

\subsection{How much does \ours{} depend on its recognisers?}
Table~\ref{tab:recog} lists each verifier's word accuracy and \ours{}' gain over the better re-recognition baseline;
Table~\ref{tab:strata} splits every test set by \emph{which} verifier reads the word correctly. Three regularities hold
on all five datasets. (i) When both verifiers read the word correctly every method is nearly perfect. (ii) When only one
verifier is right, re-recognition built on the \emph{other} verifier fails badly, while \ours{} stays accurate because it
weighs the evidence of both (IAM: 89.5\% vs.\ 25.7\% for PARSeq agreement on words only TrOCR reads, 89.0\% vs.\ 43.6\% for
TrOCR agreement on words only PARSeq reads). (iii) When neither is right, all methods degrade. \ours{}' gain is therefore
governed by how often the verifiers \emph{disagree in correctness}: it is largest on IAM, where each verifier alone is
right on only 42--65\% of words, and vanishes on IIIT5K, where PARSeq is right on 98.5\%. \ours{} is best understood as a
learned, label-efficient combination of complementary recognisers through their counterfactual evidence, not as a
substitute for a strong in-domain recogniser.

\subsection{Sensitivity to the rendering}
The ratio depends on a rendering $R(v)$ whose font never matches the document. We re-extracted all evidence with four
other fonts (including a handwriting-like one) and two other glyph sizes on fixed subsets of FUNSD, CORD and IAM (1{,}000
training and 1{,}000 test words each) and re-evaluated the claim's ratio alone, the training-free $\rho$-margin rule, and
\ours{} trained on the subset (Table~\ref{tab:fonts}). \FONTSENTENCE
